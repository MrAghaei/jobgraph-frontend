# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Job seekers who are actively looking for work and tired of checking multiple job sites. They want one place to search, compare opportunities, and understand the market before they apply. The marketing site and the product app serve the same person at different moments: discovery and conversion on the site, search and decision-making in the app. Both surfaces carry equal weight.

## Product Purpose

Jobgraph aggregates job listings in one place so seekers stop bouncing between websites. It pairs search with job-market analytics so users can decide faster and apply with more confidence. Success means less time scrolling scattered boards and more time on qualified matches backed by useful market context.

## Positioning

One place to find jobs — no need to check several websites — get analytics about the job market and decide better.

## Brand Personality

Sharp and modern. Tool-first, precise, built for people who value their time. The feel should be opinionated and fast — closer to Arc or Raycast than a traditional job board. Direct language, minimal chrome, confident data presentation. Not warm-and-fuzzy, not corporate, not playful.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages: cream backgrounds, gradient heroes, "AI-powered" eyebrows on every section
- Classic job board clutter: dense ads, noisy filters, endless list fatigue (Indeed, old LinkedIn jobs)
- Corporate enterprise UI: gray tables, stock photos, synergy energy
- Overly playful startup aesthetics: mascots, bouncy motion, cartoon illustrations

## Design Principles

1. **Respect time** — Every screen should reduce friction. If a flow adds steps without adding clarity, cut it.
2. **One place, one flow** — Search, compare, and decide without leaving Jobgraph or opening another tab.
3. **Data drives decisions** — Market analytics aren't decoration; they should change what the user applies to and when.
4. **Sharp over soft** — Tool-like precision beats comforting filler. Clarity is the kindness.
5. **Show, don't tell** — Live job counts, market stats, and real comparisons persuade better than marketing copy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target yet. Follow best practices from the start: sufficient contrast, keyboard navigation, semantic markup, and reduced-motion alternatives for any animation.
